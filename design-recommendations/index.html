PRIMARY RECOMMENDATIONBegin with large, irregular optic-white and near-black panels, a brighter ventral surface, and asymmetrical blocks that cross the body outline. Optimize by measured wet luminance—not screen color.
01

DESIGN RULES

Six decisions we can defend.

01 / FIRST PRINCIPLE

Brightness before hue

Evaluate every adjacent panel in species-weighted grayscale. A shark with one cone class can still detect strong wavelength-dependent brightness differences without conventional color vision.

2025 opsin study ↗
02 / SCALE

Use large blocks

Start with major fields approximately 15–35 cm across. Fine checks, narrow stripes, and small graphics are likely to merge at distance and in turbid water.

Shark resolution and contrast study ↗
03 / SILHOUETTE

Cross the outline

Run light–dark boundaries through the physical edges of arms, legs, shoulders, and torso. Internal decoration does not break the external silhouette.

White-shark mistaken-identity model ↗
04 / GEOMETRY

Prioritize the underside

A shark approaching from below sees the body against bright downwelling light. Test a brighter ventral field interrupted by dark structural breaks.

Great White counterillumination trial ↗
05 / FORM

Break symmetry

Make opposing limbs and shoulders different. Asymmetry creates a less continuous bilateral form and gives us a measurable alternative to regular banding.

Design hypothesis / requires behavioral testing
06 / MOTION

Design across joints

Place alternating blocks around elbows, knees, shoulders, and hips so movement produces strong temporal contrast. Conspicuity is not automatically deterrence.

Design hypothesis / requires behavioral testing
02

PANEL PLACEMENT

A first suit architecture.

SHOULDERSOne predominantly light, one predominantly dark. Carry each treatment through the outer edge.
TORSOHigh-reflectance central field cut by one broad, irregular dark diagonal. Avoid a continuous dark body mass.
ARMSTwo or three oversized blocks per arm, offset left to right and crossing the lateral silhouette.
HIPS + THIGHSUse the largest panel transitions here. Interrupt the long torso-to-leg contour when viewed from below or the side.
KNEES + CALVESOffset blocks around joints. Do not use evenly spaced zebra bands or narrow rings.
VENTRAL SURFACEFavor high reflectance with strategic dark breaks. Treat the dorsal surface as secondary to underside and side geometry.

FIRST PROTOTYPES

Three suits. One variable at a time.

PROTOTYPE A / CONTROL DIRECTION

Maximum contrast

Near-black × optic white. Large asymmetrical fields and a bright underside. This is the cleanest scientific baseline with the fewest assumptions about hue.

BUILD FIRST
PROTOTYPE B / MATERIAL TEST

Fluorescent contrast

Near-black × fluorescent yellow-green. Advance only if wet spectroscopy and underwater imaging show greater receptor-weighted separation than optic white.

MEASURE BEFORE BUILD
PROTOTYPE C / GEOMETRY TEST

Bright ventral field

A predominantly light underside with fewer dark silhouette breaks. Tests passive silhouette reduction separately from disruptive paneling.

04

AVOID FOR NOW

Attractive ideas with weak support.

  • Zebra stripesRegular narrow bands may merge at distance, and warning-pattern assumptions do not transfer reliably across shark species.
  • Blue-on-black palettesBoth may collapse toward low contrast underwater, particularly with distance and depth.
  • Human color contrastPink and cyan can look dramatically different to us while producing similar shark-weighted luminance.
  • Sea-snake mimicryTiger sharks prey on sea snakes; an assumed warning signal may not generalize to a key target species.
  • Fine graphicsBrand detail should not be confused with a functional long-range visual signal.
  • Safety languageDo not use “deterrent,” “anti-shark,” or bite-prevention claims before controlled behavioral evidence exists.
05

VALIDATION PLAN

Earn the claim in sequence.

  1. 01
    MEASURE MATERIALS

    Collect wet spectral reflectance under representative clear, coastal, turbid, and backlit illumination.

  2. 02
    MODEL THE VIEWER

    Generate Great White-, bull-, and tiger-weighted luminance images with distance, water attenuation, and spatial blur.

  3. 03
    SCREEN THE PANELS

    Remove combinations that merge at distance. Compare full silhouettes from below, side, front, and rear.

  4. 04
    TEST MOVING DECOYS

    Standardize geometry, speed, depth, and motion. Change only the visual treatment.

  5. 05
    SEPARATE BEHAVIORS

    Measure detection, orientation, approach, inspection, aborted approach, contact, and strike as different outcomes.

DESIGN STATUS

Perception-informed, not shark-proven.

A visible pattern might improve recognition, attract inspection, or produce no meaningful behavioral change. Until controlled species-relevant trials distinguish those outcomes, these recommendations define experiments—not safety claims.
