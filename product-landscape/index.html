PERSONAL SHARK-RISK PRODUCTS × EVIDENCE

KNOW THE
LANDSCAPE.

A working map of products that try to avoid exposure, change shark behavior, or reduce injury after contact. These are different jobs and should never share one vague “shark-safe” claim.

LANDSCAPE VIEWShark Stop has credible material science and limited finished-product validation. Electric deterrents currently have the strongest independent behavioral evidence. Passive visual systems remain the largest open research question.
01

THREE PRODUCT JOBS

Where does protection happen?

BEFORE ENTRY

Avoid exposure

Forecasts, alerts, tagged-shark notifications, beach closures, drones, and local knowledge help users avoid higher-risk conditions. They do not alter a nearby shark.

INFORMATION / OPERATIONS
BEFORE CONTACT

Alter behavior

Electric, magnetic, and passive visual products attempt to interrupt approach or contact. Results are technology-, species-, distance-, and context-dependent.

DETERRENCE / PERCEPTION
AFTER CONTACT

Reduce injury

Bite-resistant textiles do not prevent an encounter. They aim to reduce puncture, tearing, tissue loss, and resulting blood loss after a bite occurs.

Evidence grades reflect direct independent testing of the marketed approach—not how compelling the mechanism sounds.

Freedom+ Surf and Freedom7 have independent field evidence across Great White, tiger, and bull sharks. They reduce risk rather than eliminate it, work at short range, add equipment, and may affect usability.

WHAT IS SUPPORTED

The material resists severe damage.

A 2019 Flinders University study found Shark Stop fabric required more force to puncture and sustained smaller, shallower cuts than standard neoprene in mechanical and live Great White tests. A 2025 study found Shark Stop and three competing protective materials all reduced the area of substantial and critical damage from Great White and tiger-shark bites.

The 2025 work was funded by the NSW government and Australian Research Council. The paper states that no inventor or manufacturer funded it.

WHAT IS NOT YET SUPPORTED

A complete suit has not been fully validated.

Researchers tested material over foam bite packages—not people, limbs, circulation, or a finished suit under real surfing loads. The studies cannot establish protection from crushing injuries, fractures, vascular damage, amputation, or death. The 2025 paper calls for testing complete designs and tissue substitutes.

COMPANY MESSAGE

“Prevented 100% of critical damage” and reduced severe damage “up to 89%.”

OUR READING

The overall reduction in severe material damage is real, but these headline percentages compress species, test conditions, and categorical outcomes. Use the peer-reviewed conclusion—not a universal protection percentage.

INDEPENDENT OWNER SIGNAL

Thin sample. Mixed experience.

The clearest early Kickstarter owner report described poor sizing, short legs and torso, stiff exposed glue, limited shoulder mobility, and delivery delays. Two independent YouTube owners also documented the suit. Newer Shop reviews are more positive about warmth and construction, while still mentioning difficult entry and sizing adjustments.

THE REVIEW THAT IS STILL MISSING

Current suit × experienced surfer × repeated sessions.

  • Paddling range and shoulder fatigue
  • Warmth, flushing, and drying
  • Entry, exit, fit, and sizing consistency
  • Seam and laminate durability
  • Weight and flexibility versus a premium conventional suit
  • Protection coverage across the finished panel layout

WHITE SPACE

What Wetsuit Lab can own.

Current products mostly choose one job: deter before contact or protect after contact. The open opportunity is a wearable platform that treats visual perception, material protection, fit, and surfing performance as separate measurable layers.

Near-term wedge: build and validate the visual panel system without making deterrence claims. Long-term system: combine independently tested perception design with strategically placed bite-resistant panels—if weight, flexibility, and manufacturability remain acceptable.

IMPORTANT LIMITS

No product makes ocean use shark-safe.

Personal mitigation products reduce particular risks under particular conditions. They do not replace local warnings, environmental judgment, appropriate behavior, or emergency preparation.
